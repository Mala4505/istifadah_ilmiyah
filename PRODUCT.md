# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Event finance and admin staff (accountants, department admins) reconciling bills, budgets,
and audit data during a live event. They check the dashboard often and under time pressure —
this is a working screen glanced at between other tasks, not a leisurely report. Some staff
are older or less tech-savvy, so plain language and low cognitive load beat density or jargon.

## Product Purpose

Istifadah Ilmiyah (mumin-app) tracks ledger entries, bills, budgets, and exceptions for an
event, importing data from a Departmental export and an Audit portal, matching source
documents (OCR'd bills) to entries, and surfacing what needs human attention. Success on the
dashboard specifically: a staff member can tell, in a few seconds, whether anything needs
their attention right now and where to go to act on it.

## Brand Personality

Plain, calm, trustworthy internal ops tool — not a marketing or analytics product. Quiet
confidence: state numbers and status directly, no hype, no decorative flourishes competing
with the data.

## Anti-references

Not a generic SaaS analytics dashboard (no hero-metric gradient cards, no vanity charts, no
marketing sheen). Not a dense spreadsheet/report dump either — numbers should read at a
glance, grouped by what they mean, not just listed.

## Design Principles

- Group by meaning, not by data source — a user shouldn't need to know which view or table a
  number came from to understand why it's next to another number.
- Every number implies an action or an "all clear" — never a number with no way to tell if
  it's good or bad.
- Plain language over domain jargon in labels; save precision for the hint/subtitle line.
- Scale visual weight to urgency — exceptions and stuck work should not have the same visual
  weight as a routine daily count.
- Legible at a glance and on a phone — this is checked often, briefly, sometimes one-handed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA (contrast, keyboard, focus states — already the app's baseline). Some staff
are older/less tech-savvy: favor clear labels and larger touch targets over compact density.
Must hold up at phone width, not just desktop.
